# Statistical Analysis: Boundary Loss vs. Baseline

This document presents the full multi-seed results and pairwise statistical test matrix for the boundary-loss experiments (baseline Dice+Focal vs. fixed-schedule boundary loss vs. convergence-gated adaptive boundary loss), generated by `scripts/aggregate_results.py` and `scripts/statistical_test.py`, together with the SDF-approximation retrain-and-compare results and the held-out test-set evaluation.

## Why multi-seed, paired testing

An earlier single-seed comparison suggested the adaptive boundary-loss variant might outperform the baseline. That result did not replicate across a 5-seed batch (see below) — a concrete illustration of why single-seed comparisons are unreliable in this setting, and why every reported comparison here uses N=5 seeds (42, 123, 456, 789, 2024) with paired significance testing rather than a single run.

## Interpretation

**No pairwise comparison reaches statistical significance** at the conventional α = 0.05 threshold, for either metric, under either test. This holds across all three configuration pairs and both Dice (region overlap) and HD95 (boundary precision) — the two metric families boundary-aware loss terms are specifically intended to improve.

The differences in observed means are small relative to their standard deviations (e.g., Dice differences of ≤0.0006 against SDs of 0.0015–0.0037), and the Wilcoxon results in particular (several exactly p=1.0000 or p≥0.4) indicate the rank orderings across seeds are close to what would be expected under the null hypothesis of no effect.

This null result is not treated as a dead end — it is investigated mechanistically at the gradient level in [`gradient_analysis.md`](gradient_analysis.md), which explains *why* the boundary loss term has negligible measurable effect on training, rather than stopping at "no significant difference was found." It is then independently stress-tested three further ways: against two corrected SDF formulations (Sections "SDF-Approximation Verification" below) and against a genuinely held-out test set (Section "Held-Out Test-Set Evaluation" below).

Small nodules are segmented consistently worse than medium or large nodules across all three configs — this small < medium < large ordering holds in 13 of 15 runs individually, not just in aggregate. Boundary-aware loss is often motivated specifically as a remedy for small, hard-boundary structures, but it neither closes nor meaningfully narrows the small-nodule gap here — consistent with the gradient-magnitude explanation in `gradient_analysis.md`.

### Run Integrity Verification

Before trusting the comparisons above, all 15 result files were checked for run-to-run consistency:

- **Code version:** all 15 runs report the identical code-version fingerprint (`6d28de4f780d`), confirming no configuration drift between runs.
- **Completion:** all 15 runs completed the full 50 planned epochs; none failed or were truncated.
- **Compute cost:** mean total training time per run ranged 3,777–3,801 seconds (~63 minutes) across the three configurations; total compute across all 15 runs was approximately 15.8 GPU-hours; peak GPU memory was 9.76–9.78 GB per run.
- **HD95 skip integrity:** `hd95_skipped` (patches excluded from HD95 due to empty predictions) is exactly 0 at every run's `best_val_dice_epoch`, for all 15 runs — the reported HD95 values are never affected by skip-related bias. Nonzero skip counts (up to 22 patches in one early instance) are heaviest in the first ~10 epochs, before the model has converged, and taper to rare, isolated single-patch events for the remainder of training (occurring as late as epoch 49 in one run), never at a magnitude that affects any reported statistic.

These checks confirm the 15 runs are directly comparable and that no run-level anomaly is inflating or masking the null result reported above.

## SDF-Approximation Verification (Retrain-and-Compare)

Two known geometric approximations in the boundary-loss SDF pipeline were each quantified directly and then stress-tested by re-training both boundary-loss configurations from scratch (all 5 seeds) against a corrected SDF, and comparing paired against the original results with the same t-test + Wilcoxon methodology used above.

### Patch-Boundary Truncation

The SDF is computed within the 64³ patch with no knowledge of nodule geometry beyond the patch edge. Quantification: 304/2,651 nodules (11.5%) touch or exceed the patch boundary; mean near-boundary SDF error 0.191 mm (truncated) vs. 0.023 mm (non-truncated), whole-patch mean 0.015 mm, max 19.000 mm.

**Fixed-schedule, original vs. corrected SDF:**

| Metric | Original | Corrected | Diff | t-test p | Wilcoxon p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dice | 0.7385 ± 0.0027 | 0.7356 ± 0.0026 | −0.0029 | 0.1169 | 0.1875 |
| HD95 | 5.4018 ± 0.1660 | 5.4981 ± 0.2029 | +0.0964 | 0.4649 | 0.4375 |
| IoU | 0.6501 ± 0.0142 | 0.6397 ± 0.0127 | −0.0104 | 0.1221 | 0.1250 |

**Adaptive, original vs. corrected SDF:**

| Metric | Original | Corrected | Diff | t-test p | Wilcoxon p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dice | 0.7348 ± 0.0041 | 0.7348 ± 0.0023 | −0.0000 | 0.9933 | 1.0000 |
| HD95 | 5.5812 ± 0.2654 | 5.5813 ± 0.1739 | +0.0001 | 0.9996 | 1.0000 |
| IoU | 0.6445 ± 0.0079 | 0.6398 ± 0.0075 | −0.0048 | 0.5048 | 0.8125 |

**Verdict:** all 6 comparisons non-significant (all p > 0.11). The adaptive configuration's near-exact means (Dice and HD95 diffs of essentially zero) make this the strongest single piece of evidence that truncation is not a contributing factor.

### Rotation Approximation

Under training-time random rotation, the cached SDF is bilinear-interpolated rather than recomputed from the rotated mask. Quantification: 1,000 samples (500 nodules × 2 angle draws), whole-patch mean error 0.060 mm (max 0.388 mm), near-boundary mean error 0.240 mm (max 2.954 mm) — comparable to or larger than truncation's error.

**Fixed-schedule, original vs. rotation-corrected:**

| Metric | Original | Corrected | Diff | t-test p | Wilcoxon p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dice | 0.7385 ± 0.0027 | 0.7347 ± 0.0032 | −0.0037 | 0.1122 | 0.1250 |
| HD95 | 5.4018 ± 0.1660 | 5.4981 ± 0.1039 | +0.0963 | 0.4140 | 0.6250 |
| IoU | 0.6501 ± 0.0142 | 0.6383 ± 0.0139 | −0.0118 | 0.3489 | 0.4375 |

**Adaptive, original vs. rotation-corrected:**

| Metric | Original | Corrected | Diff | t-test p | Wilcoxon p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dice | 0.7348 ± 0.0041 | 0.7370 ± 0.0007 | +0.0022 | 0.3350 | 0.3125 |
| HD95 | 5.5812 ± 0.2654 | 5.4300 ± 0.0661 | −0.1512 | 0.2515 | 0.4375 |
| IoU | 0.6445 ± 0.0079 | 0.6411 ± 0.0145 | −0.0034 | 0.6849 | 0.6250 |

**Verdict:** all 6 comparisons non-significant (minimum p = 0.1122). Same conclusion as truncation.

**Notable secondary observation** (not causally confirmed, just observed): the adaptive config's rotation-corrected results show much tighter cross-seed variance than the originals (Dice SD 0.0041→0.0007; HD95 SD 0.2654→0.0661). A plausible explanation is that a noisier (approximated) SDF introduced seed-to-seed variability in exactly when the validation-loss-velocity-gated adaptive mechanism activates — removing that noise could make gating behavior more consistent. This is worth mentioning as an interesting aside, not asserted as proven.

**Combined caveat:** n=5 paired seeds has limited power to rule out small effects — a single p-value like 0.11–0.12 is not proof of exactly zero difference. What makes both results genuinely convincing is the *consistency* across two independent configs, two independent corrections, and three independent metrics, plus the adaptive config's near-exact match in the truncation comparison — a pattern much harder to explain as chance than any one test alone.

## Summary of All Statistical Evidence

Across this project, the same paired null result has now been observed independently four times, on four non-overlapping sets of training/evaluation runs:

1. **Original 15 runs** (validation): no significant pairwise difference on Dice, HD95, or IoU (all p > 0.24).
2. **10 truncation-corrected retrain runs**: no significant difference vs. originals on Dice, HD95, or IoU (all p > 0.11).
3. **10 rotation-corrected retrain runs**: no significant difference vs. originals on Dice, HD95, or IoU (all p > 0.11).
4. **15 test-set evaluations** (genuinely held-out data): no significant pairwise difference on Dice, HD95, IoU, or ASSD (all p > 0.17).

In total, 35 independent training runs and 15 additional held-out evaluations all converge on the same conclusion, consistent with the single mechanistic cause identified in `gradient_analysis.md`: the boundary loss term is gradient-starved at the tested weight ceiling, not incorrectly scheduled or scoped by a fixable data artifact.
